# Competitor brief

Brief JointGlow on a competitor using only public sources, each with a date.

## Scope

Confirm the competitor or market, and the reason for the brief (entering a city, a pricing decision, ad strategy). If the user gave only a city, find the 3 to 5 most relevant competitors there and say how you chose them.

## Sources to check

Note the URL and the date you checked each one:
- Website: homepage promise, conditions treated, treatments offered, calls to action, insurance and price messaging, physician credentials, locations.
- Landing pages reached from ads: offer, form fields, proof shown (reviews, studies), how fast the page gets you to booking.
- Meta Ad Library and Google Ads Transparency Center: active ads, how many there are, run dates, formats, messages, offers.
- Reviews (Google, Healthgrades, Yelp): rating, review count, recurring praise and complaints.
- News, press releases, job posts (signs of expansion), published pricing.

If the network blocks a source, say which one and continue with the others.

## Rules for evidence

- Label each point **Observed** (with source and date) or **Estimated** (with your method).
- Never present a competitor's spend, patient volume, conversions or ROAS as known unless a public source states it. Ad Library counts and run dates are observations; spend inferred from them is an estimate.
- Quote ad and page copy exactly when it matters.

## Output

1. Summary in three sentences: who they are, how they win patients, the opening for JointGlow.
2. Positioning: their promise, audience and proof.
3. Offers and pricing.
4. Advertising: channels, messages, active ad count, longest-running ads.
5. Landing page and booking path.
6. Strengths and weaknesses compared with JointGlow, using JointGlow facts only from the user or from repo and Notion sources.
7. Recommended responses, ranked.
8. Sources: a table of URL, what it showed, and the date checked.
